XIII · Nota Doctrinalis MMXXV

Mater Populi Fidelis

On 4 November 2025 the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith published a doctrinal note on the Marian titles this library documents. Here is what the Note actually teaches, what it rules out, what it leaves standing, and how this library reads in its light.

What the Note is

Full title
Mater Populi Fidelis. Doctrinal Note on Some Marian Titles Regarding Mary’s Cooperation in the Work of Salvation.
Authority
Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith · Card. Víctor Manuel Fernández, Prefect · decided in Ordinary Session 26 March 2025 · approved by Pope Leo XIV on 7 October 2025, Memorial of Our Lady of the Rosary · published 4 November 2025, Memorial of St. Charles Borromeo. A doctrinal note of the ordinary magisterium; it defines no dogma.
Text
Official English text at vatican.va · 80 paragraphs, 197 notes.

The Note presents itself as a clarification, not a curtailment. Its Presentation states that “rather than proposing limits, the present Note seeks to accompany and sustain the love of Mary and trust in her maternal intercession,” and the body adds that the document proceeds “without intending to be comprehensive or exhaustive” (§3). Its central question is the one this library was built around: “what is the meaning of Mary’s unique cooperation in the plan of salvation?” (§3).

What the Note affirms

The larger part of the document is positive Marian doctrine. Mary’s cooperation in the work of salvation is “attested to in Scripture” (§5), traced from Genesis 3:15 through the Annunciation, Cana, and Calvary to Revelation 12, and “has been taught by the Magisterium of the Church” (§13), citing the Council:

Rightly, therefore, the holy Fathers see Mary not merely as a passive instrument in the hands of God, but as freely cooperating in the work of human salvation through faith and obedience. Vatican II · Lumen Gentium §56 · quoted at Mater Populi Fidelis §13

The Note reaffirms the singular efficacy of Mary’s intercession: “her prayer for us has a value and an efficacy that cannot be compared to any other intercession” (§38). It teaches that her spiritual motherhood extends to all believers and even to all human beings (§37a), and it closes with the title it commends, and with a prayer: “Mother of the Faithful People of God, pray for us.”

Co-Redemptrix · the ruling

The Note gives the title’s history plainly (§17): “Redemptrix” is attested from the tenth century as shorthand for Mother of the Redeemer; the corrective “Co-redemptrix” first appears in an anonymous fifteenth-century hymn from the Monastery of Saint Peter in Salzburg:

Pia, dulcis et benigna… ut compassa Redemptori, captivato transgressori, tu corredemptrix fieres. Loving, sweet, and kind… as one suffering with the Redeemer for the captive sinner, you would be co-redemptrix. De compassione BMV 20 · Salzburg, 15th c. · Analecta Hymnica XLVI, n. 79 · cited at Mater Populi Fidelis n. 31 · the earliest known occurrence of the word

Popes have used the title: the first was Pius XI in 1925, and St. John Paul II employed it “on at least seven occasions” (§18), though he stopped after February 1996, when the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith examined the petition to define it as dogma. Cardinal Ratzinger’s reply to that petition was negative: “the precise meaning of these titles is not clear, and the doctrine contained in them is not mature” (§19). In 2002 he added publicly that the formula “departs to too great an extent from the language of Scripture and of the Fathers” (§19). Pope Francis opposed the title on at least three occasions (§21). The Second Vatican Council had already “refrained from using the title for dogmatic, pastoral, and ecumenical reasons” (§18). The Note then rules:

The ruling · §22
“Given the necessity of explaining Mary’s subordinate role to Christ in the work of Redemption, it is always inappropriate to use the title ‘Co-redemptrix’ to define Mary’s cooperation… When an expression requires many, repeated explanations to prevent it from straying from a correct meaning, it does not serve the faith of the People of God and becomes unhelpful.”
Mater Populi Fidelis §22 · vatican.va

The Prefect subsequently clarified, in remarks reported in the Catholic press, that the ruling bars the expression from liturgical texts and official documents of the Holy See, while private devotion is not policed (reported by Diane Montagna, 27 November 2025, and noted in Homiletic & Pastoral Review, January 2026). The doctrine the title sought to honour, Mary’s real, free, subordinate cooperation in the Redemption, is restated by the Note itself (§13, §36); what is set aside is the word.

Mediatrix · prudence, not prohibition

The treatment of “Mediatrix” is different in kind. The Note recalls that the concept appears in the Eastern Fathers from the sixth century, and that St. Andrew of Crete, St. Germanus of Constantinople, and St. John Damascene employed the title (§23). Against 1 Timothy 2:5 it states that “special prudence is required when applying the term ‘Mediatrix’ to Mary” (§24), and then concedes the legitimate sense:

The permitted sense · §25
“The word ‘mediation’ is commonly used in many areas of everyday life, where it is understood simply as cooperation, assistance, or intercession. As a result, it is inevitable that the term would be applied to Mary in a subordinate sense. Used in this way, it does not intend to add any efficacy or power to the unique mediation of Jesus Christ, true God and true man.”
Mater Populi Fidelis §25 · vatican.va

The boundary is drawn where mediation would be read into the communication of sanctifying grace itself: “any expression about her ‘mediation’ in grace must be understood as a distant analogy to Christ and his unique mediation” (§53), for “in the perfect immediacy between a human being and God in the communication of grace, not even Mary can intervene” (§54). The maximal form, “Mediatrix of All Graces,” is judged to have “limits that do not favor a correct understanding of Mary’s unique place” (§67): she could not have mediated the grace she herself received. Mary’s cooperation is located “not in the communication of grace but in her maternal intercession” (§54), which is exactly the frame the Council gave the title sixty years earlier:

Quapropter Beata Virgo in Ecclesia titulis Advocatae, Auxiliatricis, Adiutricis, Mediatricis invocatur. Quod tamen ita intelligitur, ut dignitati et efficacitati Christi unius Mediatoris nihil deroget, nihil superaddat. Therefore the Blessed Virgin is invoked in the Church under the titles of Advocate, Helper, Benefactress, and Mediatrix. This, however, is so understood that it neither takes away anything from nor adds anything to the dignity and efficacy of Christ the one Mediator. Vatican II · Lumen Gentium §62 · AAS 57 (1965) 63 · see Anthology §48 · the sense in which this library bears its name

What the Note does not say

It does not deny Mary’s cooperation in the Redemption. It teaches it (§5–15), quoting Lumen Gentium §56, and calls her “the first and foremost collaborator in the work of Redemption and grace” (§22).

It does not touch the four Marian dogmas. The divine motherhood, perpetual virginity, Immaculate Conception, and Assumption are the Note’s own foundation (§9, §14).

It does not forbid the title Mediatrix. It requires the subordinate sense of Lumen Gentium §62 and rules out only the reading that would place Mary inside the communication of sanctifying grace (§25, §53–54, §67).

It does not erase the historical record. The Note itself documents the Salzburg hymn, the papal usages, and the Eastern witnesses; what the saints and popes said remains what they said.

It does not bind private devotion. Per the Prefect’s reported clarification, the ruling governs liturgical texts and official documents of the Holy See.

This library and the Note

Mediatrix is a study library: it documents twenty centuries of witness, with provenance, in the original languages where the text is confidently fixed. That purpose is unchanged. The anthology’s records of Benedict XV (§45), Pius XI, and the medieval and patristic voices remain as written, because a library does not edit its witnesses. The Co-Redemptrix thread of the catena (20 witnesses) and the Mediatrix thread (24 witnesses) now read as the documentary history of the very question the Note has answered.

In its own voice, this library speaks as the Note and the Council speak: Mary’s mediation is maternal intercession, subordinate, derivative, and wholly ordered to her Son, taking nothing from and adding nothing to the one Mediator. The site’s name is the Lumen Gentium §62 invocation, in precisely that sense. Where the historical title Co-Redemptrix appears in these pages, it appears as what it now is: a chapter in the history of doctrine, documented from its Salzburg origin to its magisterial retirement in 2025. The Defense page’s treatment of the objection has been annotated accordingly.
